02 — Effects

What a warmer planet is already changing

These aren't distant forecasts. Weather systems, coastlines, and ecosystems are responding to warming that has already happened, and the pace has picked up noticeably since the 1980s.

  • More frequent extreme weather

    Heatwaves, droughts, wildfires, and intense rainfall are all becoming more common and more severe. Warmer air holds more moisture, which fuels heavier storms even as other regions dry out for longer stretches.

    5x more heatwaves than the 1950s

  • Rising sea levels

    Warmer water expands, and melting glaciers and ice sheets add more volume to the oceans. Low-lying coastal cities and small island nations face growing flood risk and, over time, permanent loss of land.

    21 cm average rise since 1900

  • Ocean warming & acidification

    Oceans absorb most of the excess heat and around a quarter of the carbon dioxide emitted. That's slowed warming on land, but it's raising ocean temperatures and acidity, bleaching coral reefs and stressing marine food chains.

    ~90% of excess heat absorbed by oceans

  • Biodiversity & habitat loss

    Shifting temperatures and rainfall patterns are moving faster than many species can adapt or migrate. Habitats are shrinking and fragmenting at the same time, compounding the pressure.

    69% drop in monitored wildlife since 1970

  • Human health & livelihoods

    Heat stress, changing disease patterns, food and water insecurity, and displacement from extreme weather all fall hardest on communities with the fewest resources to adapt, despite contributing the least to emissions.

    3.3B+ people in highly climate-vulnerable regions

Every effect on this page compounds the others — a warmer ocean fuels bigger storms, and a drier land burns more easily.

None of this is fixed. It's still being decided.

The gap between the worst-case and the manageable-case future comes down to choices being made right now.
