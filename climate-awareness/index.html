The basics

What is climate change, really?

Every day, sunlight passes through Earth's atmosphere, warms the surface, and radiates back out as heat. Certain gases — carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide — trap some of that heat instead of letting it escape. This is the greenhouse effect, and it's the reason Earth is warm enough to support life at all.

The problem isn't the greenhouse effect itself. It's that burning fossil fuels, clearing forests, and industrial agriculture have pushed greenhouse gas concentrations far higher than at any point in at least 800,000 years, so more heat is trapped than the planet can safely absorb.
