03 — Solutions

What actually moves the needle

Individual choices matter, but the biggest gains come from decisions made at the scale of energy grids, transport systems, and land use. Real progress uses both at once.

Systemic change

The shift already underway

Renewable electricity — mainly solar and wind — is now the cheapest source of new power generation in most of the world, which is why it's being built faster than any energy source in history. Pairing that with electrified transport and more efficient buildings covers the largest share of the emissions cuts needed this decade.

Protecting and restoring forests, wetlands, and soils adds a second lever: these ecosystems can pull carbon back out of the atmosphere rather than only avoiding new emissions.

Where to start

Everyday actions with real impact

These won't solve the problem alone, but they add up, and several of them also shape demand for cleaner systems.

  • Rethink transport Walking, cycling, public transit, or an electric vehicle can cut personal transport emissions dramatically, especially for daily commutes.
  • Cut food waste Around a third of all food produced is wasted, and wasted food still carries the full emissions cost of growing, transporting, and disposing of it.
  • Shift your plate Eating less red meat and dairy — even a few days a week — meaningfully lowers the methane and land-use footprint of your diet.
  • Improve home efficiency Better insulation, efficient appliances, and switching to electric heating reduce both emissions and energy bills over time.
  • Choose renewable energy Where available, switching your electricity plan or adding solar panels sends direct demand toward cleaner power generation.
  • Use your voice and vote Policy shapes the systems everyone operates within. Supporting climate-minded policy and leaders scales far beyond individual habits.

The most effective action most people can take is treating this as a shared, solvable problem — and acting like it.
