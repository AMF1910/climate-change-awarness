01 — Causes

Where the emissions actually come from

Climate change isn't caused by one industry or one habit. It's the combined result of how the modern world produces energy, grows food, and uses land. Here's roughly how the major sources compare, based on widely published global emissions breakdowns.

  • Burning fossil fuels for energy

    Coal, oil, and natural gas burned for electricity, heating, and transport release carbon dioxide that had been locked underground for millions of years. This single category is the largest source of global greenhouse gas emissions by a wide margin.

    ~73% of global GHG emissions

  • Agriculture & livestock

    Raising livestock produces methane; synthetic fertilizers release nitrous oxide; and rice paddies emit methane as they flood. Methane and nitrous oxide trap far more heat per molecule than CO₂, even in smaller quantities.

    ~12% of global GHG emissions

  • Deforestation & land-use change

    Forests absorb carbon dioxide as they grow. When they're cleared for farmland, grazing, or timber, that stored carbon is released, and the planet loses one of its most effective natural carbon sinks at the same time.

    ~11% of global GHG emissions

  • Industrial processes

    Making cement, steel, and chemicals releases greenhouse gases directly from the chemical reactions involved, separate from the energy used to power the factories. Cement production alone is a significant single source.

    ~5% of global GHG emissions

  • Waste & landfills

    Organic waste breaking down without oxygen in landfills produces methane. Wastewater treatment adds a smaller but still measurable share of global emissions.

    ~3% of global GHG emissions

Roughly three-quarters of all emissions trace back to a single choice: how we produce and use energy.
